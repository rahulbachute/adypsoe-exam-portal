CIE-II · CCE ASSESSMENTS

Planning CCE assessment

CIE-II is the Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) component of CIE and covers Units 3, 4 and 5, with a weightage of 24 marks. Faculty should plan assessment activities to suit the Course Outcomes (COs), the cognitive level targeted and the nature of the subject content.

The ADYPSOE Rubric Manual 2026–27 provides a common four-level analytic rubric approach to support transparent, consistent and defensible assessment. Share the relevant rubric and performance expectations with students before conducting the assessment.

Common four-level analytic scale

LevelPerformanceGeneral interpretation
4ExcellentOutcome fully achieved; accurate, independent and well-developed performance.
3GoodOutcome substantially achieved; minor errors or gaps.
2SatisfactoryOutcome partially achieved; requires improvement or guidance.
1Needs ImprovementOutcome minimally achieved or not achieved.

Record the level (1–4) against each criterion, not merely the final score. This supports CO-wise attainment where an assessment tool maps to more than one CO.

Assessment tools covered in the Rubric Manual

  • Quiz / Class Test
  • Assignment / Tutorial
  • Laboratory / Practical
  • Programming / Coding
  • Case Study
  • Mini Project / Project
  • Seminar / Presentation
  • Viva Voce
  • Group Discussion / Debate
  • Design Challenge / Hackathon

Choose the rubric that best matches the assessment tool and the cognitive level of the mapped CO. The manual permits contextualisation of a criterion name or adjustment of weights within ±5% by the course coordinator with Department Academic/OBE Committee approval, while retaining the four-level structure and 100% total weight.

Computation of rubric scores

For each criterion, award a performance level from 1 to 4 and multiply it by the criterion weight expressed as a decimal. Sum the weighted levels to obtain a Weighted Rubric Score on a 1–4 scale.

Marks Awarded = (Weighted Rubric Score ÷ 4) × Maximum Marks for the Component

Example from the manual: For a component carrying 20 marks and a Weighted Rubric Score of 3.4, marks awarded = (3.4 ÷ 4) × 20 = 17 marks.

Where one rubric-based tool maps to multiple COs, attribute relevant criteria to each CO according to the CO–assessment mapping defined in the course file.

Bloom’s Taxonomy and question framing

Use the revised Bloom’s Taxonomy levels L1–L6 to align assessment questions with the cognitive demand of the mapped CO.

LevelCognitive processIndicative action verbs
L1RememberDefine, state, list, identify, recall
L2UnderstandExplain, describe, summarize, interpret, compare
L3ApplyApply, solve, calculate, demonstrate, implement
L4AnalyzeAnalyze, differentiate, examine, investigate, correlate
L5EvaluateEvaluate, justify, critique, assess, validate
L6CreateDesign, develop, formulate, construct, propose

OBE reminder: The action verb alone does not determine the Bloom’s level. Classify the complete question by the thinking required in the expected answer. Before finalising a paper, map every question/sub-question to the relevant CO and verify BTL level, syllabus alignment, marks, expected answer depth and CO-wise/BTL-wise weightage against the approved blueprint.

Question paper setting, moderation & examination duties

Approved formats, syllabus coverage requirements, moderation processes and submission deadlines will be shared through authorised institutional channels. Examiner appointments, practical/oral schedules, evaluation instructions and marks submission guidelines will be notified separately.

Security: Confidential question papers, answer keys, student marks and restricted examination material must not be uploaded to this public website or public repository.
