CONTINUOUS INTERNAL EVALUATION · CIE-II

CCE Assessment Guidelines

Guidance for planning, conducting, evaluating and documenting Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) assessments for autonomous courses.

CIE-II · 24 marks

Scope and weightage

CIE-II is the Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE) component of CIE. It is based on Units 3, 4 and 5 and carries a total weightage of 24 marks.

Faculty should plan the CCE assessment activities across the prescribed units and align each activity with the relevant Course Outcomes (COs), expected cognitive level and subject-specific learning requirements. The 24 marks represent the total CIE-II contribution; the allocation of marks among individual activities should follow the approved course assessment plan.

STEP 1 · PLAN THE ASSESSMENT

Align assessment with Course Outcomes

  1. Review the Course Outcomes and the content of Units 3, 4 and 5.
  2. Identify the COs assessed by each proposed CCE activity.
  3. Determine the cognitive level (L1–L6) expected from students.
  4. Select an assessment tool that genuinely measures the intended learning outcome.
  5. Prepare the activity instructions, marks allocation, criteria and evaluation plan before communicating the activity to students.

Assessment methods should be selected according to the intended learning and the nature of the task. A question's Bloom’s level depends on the thinking required in the expected answer, not simply on the action verb used.

STEP 3 · APPLY THE ANALYTIC RUBRIC

Common four-level performance scale

LevelPerformanceInterpretation
4ExcellentOutcome fully achieved; accurate, independent and well-developed performance.
3GoodOutcome substantially achieved; minor errors or gaps.
2SatisfactoryOutcome partially achieved; requires improvement or guidance.
1Needs ImprovementOutcome minimally achieved or not achieved.

Share the relevant rubric with students before the assessment. Apply the criterion weights specified in the rubric; the weights within each rubric total 100%. Record the level awarded against each criterion, rather than recording only the final score.

STEP 4 · COMPUTE MARKS

Rubric score calculation

For each criterion, multiply the level awarded (1–4) by its criterion weight expressed as a decimal. Add the weighted values to obtain the Weighted Rubric Score on a scale of 1 to 4.

Marks Awarded = (Weighted Rubric Score ÷ 4) × Maximum Marks for the Component

Illustration from the Rubric Manual: If the maximum marks for an assessment component are 20 and the Weighted Rubric Score is 3.4, then marks awarded = (3.4 ÷ 4) × 20 = 17 marks.

Apply this calculation to the maximum marks assigned to the particular assessment component. The aggregate CIE-II score must remain within its prescribed total of 24 marks, in accordance with the approved course assessment plan.

STEP 5 · MAP TO BLOOM’S TAXONOMY

Use the revised taxonomy (L1–L6)

LevelCognitive processIndicative question words
L1RememberDefine, state, list, identify, recall
L2UnderstandExplain, describe, summarize, interpret, compare
L3ApplyApply, solve, calculate, demonstrate, implement
L4AnalyzeAnalyze, differentiate, examine, investigate, correlate
L5EvaluateEvaluate, justify, critique, assess, validate
L6CreateDesign, develop, formulate, construct, propose

Use the complete question and expected answer to determine the cognitive level. For example, “compare” may require understanding or analysis depending on the task; “design” is L6 when students create a solution rather than reproduce a taught one.

STEP 6 · DOCUMENT AND REVIEW

Faculty documentation checklist

  • Maintain the CCE assessment plan for Units 3–5 and ensure its total contribution is 24 marks.
  • Record the CO mapping and Bloom’s Taxonomy level for each assessment activity.
  • Retain the activity question/instructions and the rubric shared with students.
  • Record criterion-wise levels and computed marks.
  • Where one tool maps to multiple COs, attribute relevant criteria to each CO as defined in the course file.
  • Retain completed rubric sheets as evidence in the course file for internal academic audit and accreditation.
  • Complete marks entry, verification and submission within the schedule notified by the Examination Section.

The Rubric Manual permits contextualisation, such as renaming a criterion or adjusting weights within ±5%, with approval of the Department Academic/OBE Committee, provided the four-level structure and total weight of 100% are retained.

Applicability and authority

Follow approved course-level instructions

This page provides implementation guidance based on the supplied ADYPSOE reference documents. It does not prescribe a marks split among individual CCE activities. The course-level activity plan, assessment allocation and any additional instructions must be approved through the institute's prescribed academic process. Where a formally approved regulation or circular differs, the applicable approved document will prevail.
