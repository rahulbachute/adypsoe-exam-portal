Faculty Examination Guidelines

Operational guidance for course coordinators, faculty members and departmental examination coordinators under ADYPSOE Rules and Regulations R-26.

R-26 · Version 1.0

Purpose and applicability

This page summarises faculty-facing provisions from the Institute’s Rules and Regulations for Examination and Evaluation, R-26 / Version 1.0, effective AY 2026–27. It is intended as a practical reference for assessment design and examination responsibilities. Faculty must verify the applicability of autonomy to the programme, admission batch and semester concerned.

The approved regulations, Academic Council decisions, Examination Cell circulars, appointment orders and course-specific approved assessment scheme remain authoritative. This page does not replace prescribed templates, confidential instructions or official communications.

The MSE allocation described in R-26 is 10 raw marks for Unit 1 and 10 raw marks for Unit 2, converted proportionately to 8 marks per unit in the 16-mark CIE allocation. The SEE carries 60 marks, with 12 marks allocated to each of Units 1 to 5.

Passing thresholds for theory courses are CIE ≥16/40, SEE ≥24/60 and combined CIE+SEE ≥40/100. All three requirements must be met. No separate passing requirement applies to MSE or CCE individually.

02 · MID-SEMESTER EXAMINATION

MSE planning and conduct

  • The MSE is conducted at department level in accordance with the Academic Calendar and the dates assigned/notified by the Examination Cell.
  • The detailed MSE/internal assessment schedule is to be published or announced by the Deans/HODs at least two weeks before the scheduled examination.
  • The Department Examination Coordinator coordinates the conduct of MSE and related examinations, prepares and circulates the timetable, and makes the necessary seating arrangements.
  • Where a course is common to different programmes, the examination coordinator should coordinate a common date and time as directed in the manual and by the Examination Section.
  • The department should submit the MSE absent report on a daily basis as per the notified examination schedule.
  • Faculty should evaluate promptly, provide constructive feedback and show answer papers to students as specified in R-26.

The detailed question-paper pattern, duration, question-wise distribution and internal options must be taken from the current approved format or specific Examination Cell instructions; this page does not prescribe them.

03 · COMPREHENSIVE CONTINUOUS EVALUATION

CCE design and implementation

CCE carries 24 marks and covers Units 3 to 5. It is conducted continuously by the course coordinator/faculty during the semester. The assessment design and mark allocation must follow the approved course scheme.

  • Select assessment tools based on the Course Outcomes, intended cognitive levels, nature of the course and learning activity.
  • Ensure that assessment tools are consistent with the approved course scheme and OBE requirements.
  • Use appropriate predefined criteria/rubrics and communicate task expectations to students.
  • Assess submissions consistently and provide feedback that helps students understand their performance and areas for improvement.
  • Maintain records of the MSE and continuous assessment tools used. The manual specifies that these records are to be duly signed by the Head of Department.
  • CCE may be periodically monitored by the BoS/authorised academic committee, as required.

04 · OBE ALIGNMENT

CO mapping and Bloom’s Taxonomy

  • Every course should have measurable Course Outcomes (COs) aligned with course content.
  • Questions and assessment tasks should assess the intended COs and appropriate cognitive levels.
  • For SEE, questions should be mapped to relevant COs and provide balanced cognitive-level coverage in accordance with the approved blueprint.
  • Use the revised Bloom’s Taxonomy: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyse, Evaluate and Create (L1–L6).
  • Ensure constructive alignment among the approved curriculum, COs, assessment methods and expected learning outcomes.

05 · SEE QUESTION PAPER SETTING

Paper setter responsibilities

Question papers for SEE must be prepared in accordance with the prescribed pattern, template and approved question-paper blueprint.

  • Set questions strictly within the scope of the approved syllabus.
  • Design questions to assess intended COs at appropriate Bloom’s cognitive levels, with suitable emphasis on application, analysis, evaluation and creation wherever appropriate.
  • Conform to the approved question-paper blueprint and prescribed marks/coverage.
  • Submit the number of paper sets specified in the appointment letter, along with the prescribed marking scheme/model answer scheme.
  • Specify any charts, tables, IS codes, data books or other permitted reference material required for the examination.
  • Check the final printout carefully for question text, diagrams, figures, marks, equations and page layout before sealing and submission as instructed.
  • Submit the required declaration/undertaking and follow the instructions issued by the Examination Section.
  • Maintain strict confidentiality and do not disclose the appointment or any question-paper content.

R-26 states a general SEE pattern of 60 marks and maximum duration of 2 hours 30 minutes, while allowing course-specific duration where specified in the approved curriculum. Follow the course curriculum and current Examination Cell instructions.

06 · MODERATION AND EVALUATION

Moderation and marking consistency

  • Paper setters and moderators are appointed through the approved examination governance process. BoS recommends panels, BoE approves panels, and the CoE issues appointment orders and administers the process.
  • Moderators must follow the approved moderation process and sampling/thresholds notified by the Examination Cell.
  • Where more than one assessor evaluates a course, the course coordinator/paper setter chairperson should conduct an assessor meeting to explain the model answer scheme and marking scheme to promote uniformity.
  • SEE answer books are assessed offline at the Central Assessment Programme (CAP) by the approved panel under Examination Cell procedures.
  • Any discrepancy in a question paper that may affect assessment is handled through the process and competent committee/authority identified in R-26; faculty should report it through the prescribed channel rather than independently changing the scheme.

Do not publish or circulate confidential question papers, model answers, live assessment records or internal moderation material on this public portal.

07 · TERM WORK, PRACTICAL AND ORAL

Assessment and documentation

  • Where Term Work is prescribed, assess the specified activities and submissions using predefined criteria/rubrics and the approved course scheme.
  • Assessment may consider work performed, submission quality, timely completion, attendance and understanding, as applicable to the course.
  • Students’ practical journals should document assignments, observations, results, reflections and other relevant material; entries should be clearly labelled and dated.
  • The Course Coordinator assesses Term Work and submits the final grade to the CoE office at the end of the semester.
  • Practical/Oral examinations are conducted jointly by one Internal and one External Examiner. R-26 specifies 50% weightage for each examiner in the joint evaluation.
  • The Internal and External Examiners jointly prepare and authenticate the mark/grade sheet and submit it to the Examination Cell within the prescribed time.
  • Students must secure at least 40% in each prescribed Term Work/Practical/Oral evaluation head, subject to the approved course scheme.

08 · DEPARTMENT EXAMINATION COORDINATION

Department Examination Coordinator (DEC)

The HoD acts as Examination Coordinator or may nominate a senior faculty member, with the Director’s approval, for one academic year. The name is communicated to the Examination Cell.

R-26 assigns the coordinator responsibilities including:

  • Coordinating MSE, Practical, Viva Voce, IPE/Prelims and related examinations.
  • Preparing and displaying departmental timetables and circulating them to course teachers and concerned departments.
  • Coordinating common-course examinations across programmes where required.
  • Making necessary seating arrangements and examination logistics.
  • Arranging question-paper setting for departmental examinations.
  • Informing an out-of-department course teacher of the examination schedule at least eight days in advance, as stated in the manual.
  • Arranging required examination stationery and collecting/acknowledging receipt of blank answer books and formats from the Examination Section.
  • Supporting orderly conduct of examinations and reporting incidents through the prescribed channel.

09 · MARKS, RECORDS AND TIMELINES

Submission and record maintenance

  • Internal marks, continuous assessment, practical and viva-voce marks/grades are to be uploaded in ERP by the concerned faculty within one week, as stated in R-26.
  • Submit finalised internal marks and attendance through the HOD/departmental process within the timelines notified by the Examination Cell.
  • Maintain assessment records, rubrics, MSE answer papers and CCE evidence in the prescribed manner. R-26 requires records of MSE and continuous assessment tools to be duly signed by the HOD.
  • Protect confidentiality of student assessment data, examination records, question papers and all restricted examination material.
  • Use only approved institutional systems and authorised channels for transmitting marks and examination records.

10 · EXAMINATION DUTIES AND CONDUCT

Supervision and examination integrity

Faculty appointed to examination duties must follow the duty order and instructions issued by the CoE/Examination Section. The Examination Centre Committee supports operational readiness, seating, custody and safe handling of examination material, attendance and absentee reporting, deployment of supervisory staff and incident reporting under CoE supervision.

Report suspected unfair means, irregularities, question-paper discrepancies or examination incidents promptly through the prescribed institutional channel. Malpractice cases are handled through the approved LMECRC process and competent authority; faculty should not independently adjudicate such cases.
